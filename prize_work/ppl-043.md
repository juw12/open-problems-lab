## Statement

For every positive integer m, find consecutive primes pₖ,…,pₙ (k < n) whose
alternating sum

    m = pₙ − pₙ₋₁ + ⋯ + (−1)ⁿ⁻ᵏ pₖ

equals m, with the primes constrained by Sun's stated bounds: the primes are
NOT EXCEEDING `2m + 2.2√m` (m even) or `m + 4.6√m` (m odd).

## The method (prefix-sum identity)

Define alternating prefix sums s₀ = 0, sᵢ = pᵢ − sᵢ₋₁ (i ≥ 1), i.e.
sᵢ = pᵢ − pᵢ₋₁ + ⋯ + (−1)ⁱ⁻¹p₁. Every alternating sum over the block
p_{j+1},…,pᵢ (j = k−1, 1 ≤ j < i) is:

- m = sᵢ − sⱼ  if i − j is even (same parity),
- m = sᵢ + sⱼ  if i − j is odd (opposite parity).

The parity subsequences are strictly increasing: s₀ < s₂ < s₄ < ⋯ and
s₁ < s₃ < s₅ < ⋯ (successive gaps are differences of twin primes > 0).
So for fixed block length l = i − j: odd l ⇔ sum case, even l ⇔ difference
case, and each l gives an O(N) vectorized pass. (Verified: 10 = s₇−s₁,
1 = s₂−s₀; s₁..s₁₆ = 2,1,4,3,8,5,12,7,16,13,18,19,22,21,26,27 = OEIS A008347.)

## Why it is hard

- Conjectured, not proved: it is a statement about every m, and the bound
  grows only like 2m (resp. m for odd m), i.e. the required top prime is O(m)
  — much smaller than the O(m log m) scale where elementary density arguments
  become trivial. Proving existence of a representation *inside* the bound is
  the open part.
- Bound-tight cases exist at small m (303, 2382 hit the bound exactly), so any
  proof needs fine-grained control, not just density.
- Verification alone is not the prize; but a tenfold verification extension is
  a real, citable partial result (and a live counterexample hunt).

## Feasibility for a laptop

The prefix-sum identity makes verification embarrassingly vectorizable:
sieve to 2·10⁸+margin in ~5 s (numpy odd-only sieve), prefix sums in ~4 s,
and a full 10⁸-range length sweep in O(L·N) vector ops. Memory ≈ 1.3 GB.
One hour of single-thread CPU is ample for m ≤ 10⁸ with the length-sweep +
residual method below. Feasible.

## RESULTS (filled after runs, 2026-11-10)

Method as implemented: a refinement of option 1 — instead of a length sweep,
a **per-m exact min-top "window method"** (`verify_all.py`), using the parity
structure of the prefix sums:

- sᵢ even ⟺ (i odd or i = 0); sᵢ odd ⟺ i even ≥ 2. So E = {s at even
  indices} = {0} ∪ {odd values}, O = {s at odd indices} = {even values ≥ 2}.
- Every representation of m has top pₙ ≥ m (pᵢ = sᵢ + sᵢ₋₁), so only a narrow
  top window needs checking.
- **Odd m**: top in [m, m + 4.6√m] (≈ 40 primes at 10⁸). Per candidate i there
  is at most one j (sⱼ = m − sᵢ or sᵢ − m, membership test O(1)); ascending-i
  first hit = exact min top.
- **Even m**: exhaustive case split → K1 (m ∈ O, j = 0), D1 (sᵢ = m + ev[c],
  c ≥ 1 ascending), D2 (sᵢ = m + od[d], d ≥ 0 ascending); sum cases with
  j ≥ 2 are parity-impossible. Expected ~30 candidates per case (ev/od
  density ≈ 1/30). First hit = exact min top.

**Gate (≤ 5 min): PASS.** `verify_all.py 1 200000 gate` in ~1 s: 0 violations;
all 4 slide examples reproduced exactly (10→17, 20→41, 303→383, 2382→4871);
all 1013 (m, min-top, bound) triples of the earlier independent exact run
(`search.py`, M = 2·10⁵, `out_200000.json`) match to the value. The published
10⁷ bound is re-verified inside the main run (0 violations for m ≤ 10⁷).

**Main run: m = 1 … 10⁸ — 0 violations out of 100,000,000.** Two chunks

### The two statistics, and why t/B was the wrong one

The allowance is itself proportional to sqrt(m): A(m) = 2.2*sqrt(m) (m even),
4.6*sqrt(m) (m odd). So with base = 2m (even) / m (odd),

    gap(m) = B(m) - t(m),   w(m) = gap(m)/sqrt(m),   e(m) = A - w = (t - base)/sqrt(m)

`w` is the margin in units of sqrt(m); `e` is the deficit of the minimal top
prime above the baseline, and the conjecture is exactly e(m) <= A. The ratio
t/B = 1 - gap/B ~ 1 - w/(2*sqrt(m)) is a strictly decreasing function of w/√m
inside a decade and tends to 1 automatically, so a "record t/B" carries no
information beyond the record gap, and its numerical value is fixed by 1/sqrt(m)
to within 1e-6 (at m = 257787004, gap 34689 = 2.16*sqrt(m), so the ratio can only
be 1 - 1.08/sqrt(m) = 0.9999327 - it *is* the arithmetic, not a signal).

Four findings, all empirical:

1. **No null sequence violates the bound.** 26 sequences x 100,000 m = 2.6M exact
   min-top values, **0 violations** (and 0 in the real bands). Sun's bound is not
   something the primes just barely manage; it is satisfied with the same order of
   comfort by a random set of the same density.
2. **The odd-side margin is generic.** The real odd-side maxima sit inside or below the
   null range at both bands (1e8: 0.1682 vs nulls 0.1534-0.2591, median 0.1934;
   1e7: 0.3629, **below all 12 nulls**, whose range is 0.3741-0.6260). The *median*
   odd-side allowance used agrees with the nulls to three decimals
   (u_med = 0.0021 real vs 0.0020-0.0022 null). So the typical odd-m deficit needs no
   prime-specific explanation at all: it is exactly what a random covering gives.
3. **The even side is where the primes are measurably tighter** - and only just.
   At 1e8 the real even e_max = -0.4016 lies **above all 12 null values**
   (-9.45 ... -0.478): the tightest of the 13 sequences. But the null spread is
   twentyfold and the margin of the separation is 0.08*sqrt(m), so this is a hint,
   not evidence; at 1e7 the real value (-3.17) is mid-range. Note this is the
   *opposite* direction to the intuition "primes are nicer than random": on the even
   side the primes come closer to the bound than a random ordering of their own gaps.
4. **The even-side spread is consistent with a hitting-position effect, and is not
   explained by gap variance.**
   Gap statistics in [1e8, 2e8]: real mean 18.806, var 261.2, lag-1 corr -0.033,
   pair-gap var 505.2; permutation 18.05, var 239.6, corr ~0, pair-var 479.1;
   Cramer 18.79, var 315.8, corr ~0, pair-var 631.7. The null even e_max values are
   *not* ordered by any of these (the Cramer model, with 21% more gap variance than
   real, is not systematically more extreme than the permutation model), so the
   even-side margin is set by where the walk s_i - p_i/2 first crosses m, not by the
   local gap statistics. A proof cannot get the even side from a variance argument.
   [Referee lab_review_261118_12 wording fix: rank statistics over the 12 nulls are
   Spearman(e_max, gap var) = +0.22 and Spearman(e_max, lag-1 corr) = +0.63 at 1e8, but
   every null's lag-1 corr lies in [-0.0008, +0.0004] (all noise), so neither statistic
   orders the spread and the "first crossing" reading is an absence-of-ordering
   argument on 6 replicates per model — consistent with, not demonstrated as, the

Programme reading for the prize: the 16-31x comfort measured by 261118_6 is *not* an
arithmetic accident on the odd side (finding 2), while on the even side the primes sit
at the tight edge of what their own gap multiset allows (finding 3). A proof must
handle the even/diff case, where the margin is smallest and where the truth is
*tighter* than the random model - and it cannot be replaced by a density/covering
argument, because such an argument would prove a strictly weaker (though true) statement.

### Verification

`verify_null043.py` is a separate implementation (own sieve, own prefix sums, own
per-m minimal-top that scans the whole allowed range m <= p_i <= B(m) with explicit
index bounds, no window, no class sweep, no active sets) and rebuilds the null
sequences from the rules recorded in `null_out/null043.json`. All five checks PASS:
A published examples 4/4 (17, 41, 383, 4871); B 400/400 at 1e7 and 100/100 at 1e8
against the committed t-arrays; C the recorded worst-deficit m of each parity at 1e8
re-derived exactly (t and e); D 40/40 sampled m per rebuilt permutation and Cramer
sequence; E the deficit maxima recomputed from the committed arrays, exact to 1e-6.

The verifier earned its keep: its first run disagreed with the committed arrays on
121/2000 values at 1e7 and exposed a missing case in the engine - a representation
whose **top prime equals m** (the length-1 block m = p_i, which exists whenever m is
prime), excluded by a searchsorted with side='right'. The rigorous start is p_i >= m
(p_i = m is possible, p_i < m is not: p_i = s_i + s_{i-1} > s_i + s_j = m unless
j = i-1). After the fix the two engines agree on 200,000 values and **no reported
number changed** (both band starts, 1e7+1 = 11 x 909091 and 1e8+1 = 17 x 5882353, are
composite). The fix is in the committed code.

### STEP 4 — KILL TEST (<= 5 min)

`witness043.py --kill`: two 1e6 bands (2,001 and 20,001 m). Kill criteria: (a) the
minimal counted top must equal the committed t(m) elementwise, else the counting is
wrong; (b) if R(m) were 1 nearly everywhere the dataset would carry no information.

The kill test also found a real gap in the *published* engine's arithmetic: blocks
that contain the prime 2 (j = 0) break the parity rule m = (i-j) mod 2, because 2 is
the only even prime. For those blocks the alternating sum is m = s_i exactly, in
either parity. The two-case sweeps miss every such block whose parity differs from
i's, which is why m = 1 and m = 2 came out empty before the fix and why the committed
t-arrays alone never expose it (they are anchored at m >= 1 with generic witnesses
available). `zero_family()` in witness043.py adds exactly those members.
