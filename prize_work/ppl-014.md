## Statement

Let \(A\subset(1,\infty)\) be countably infinite and suppose that for every
ordered pair \(x\ne y\) in \(A\) and every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
|kx-y|\ge1.
\]
Does this imply that \(A\) is sparse? In particular, must
\[
\sum_{x\in A}\frac1{x\log x}<\infty
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sum_{\substack{x<n\\x\in A}}\frac1x=o(\log n)?
\]

## Prize and current status

The documented offer is **US$500**, attributed to Paul Erdős / the
Combinatorics Foundation in [Erdős (1997), “Some of my favorite problems and

The second displayed assertion is established positively by Koukoulopoulos,
Lamzouri, and Lichtman (KLL25): their theorem says a set with positive
limsup logarithmic reciprocal density contains distinct \(\alpha,\beta\)
with \(|m\alpha-\beta|<\varepsilon\) for some positive integer \(m\), for
every \(\varepsilon>0\). Taking \(\varepsilon=1\) and contraposing the
separation hypothesis gives the stated \(o(\log n)\) bound.

For the first assertion, the problem page links Mattia Apicella's 2026
rational counterexample and Lean formalization, frozen at commit
[`0f4b969`](https://github.com/mapice/erdos-143-candidate-disproof/tree/0f4b96940c97c7e0c36d17b584ec2bf1d842c02a).
The associated proof claim says independent mathematical review and external
acceptance are pending; the repository itself calls this a candidate. Thus
this is an important claimed negative answer, not an independently reviewed
resolution. The open label and pending claim should not be conflated.

## What is known

- For integer \(A\), the condition is exactly primitivity: if one integer
  divides another, some \(k\) gives \(kx=y\); otherwise every nonzero
  integer difference has absolute value at least one.
- Erdős [Er35] proved
  \(\sum_{n\in A}1/(n\log n)<\infty\) for integer primitive sets.
- Behrend [Be35] proved an upper bound of order
  \(\log x/\sqrt{\log\log x}\) for the partial reciprocal sum; Erdős,
  Sárközy, and Szemerédi [ESS67] improved this to \(o(\log x)\). The
  erdosproblems.com page's displayed Behrend formula omits the condition
  \(n\in A\); a July 2026 comment explicitly flags that omission.
- Haight's result, recalled by Erdős [Er73, Er77c], gives zero natural density
  under the stronger hypothesis that distinct elements are independent over
  \(\mathbb Q\).
- KLL25 resolves the second question for the real-valued setting. It does not
  imply convergence of the reciprocal-logarithmic series.
- The two comments on the current problem thread (22 July 2026 and 24 April
  2026) are a typo correction and an unreviewed proposed flow argument for
  the integer case; neither changes the status of the real-valued question.
